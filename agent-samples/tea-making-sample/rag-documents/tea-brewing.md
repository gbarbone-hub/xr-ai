# Tea brewing reference

Prefer brewing instructions printed on the tea package. When package
instructions are unavailable, use these general starting points and adjust to
taste.

| Tea style | Water temperature | Steep time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Black tea | 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) | 4 minutes |
| Green tea | 176 °F (80 °C) | 2 minutes |
| White tea | 176 °F (80 °C) | 3 minutes |
| Oolong tea | 194 °F (90 °C) | 3 minutes |
| Pu-erh tea | 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) | 4 minutes |
| Herbal infusion | 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) | 5 minutes |

Use fresh water. A common ratio is one tea bag or 2–3 grams of loose tea per
240–300 millilitres of water. Green and white teas can become bitter when the
water is too hot or the steep is too long. Herbal infusions need a full boil.

## Example varieties

- Earl Grey, English breakfast, and other breakfast black teas, including
  decaffeinated: 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) for 4 minutes.
- Jasmine green and genmaicha: 176 °F (80 °C) for 2 minutes.
- White peony: 176 °F (80 °C) for 3 minutes.
- Moroccan mint or chamomile: 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) for 5 minutes.
- Rooibos: 212 °F (100 °C, boiling) for 5 minutes.

For iced tea, brew roughly double strength before pouring over ice. For cold
brew, refrigerate tea in cool water for 6–12 hours and remove the leaves or
bags before serving.
